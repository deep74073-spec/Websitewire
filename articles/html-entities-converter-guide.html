HTML Entity Encoder and Decoder: A Complete Beginner's Guide

HTML documents use special characters such as angle brackets and ampersands to define markup. When you want to display these characters as ordinary text, you may need to convert them into HTML entities. Websitewire's free HTML Entity Encoder and Decoder makes this process simple.

What are HTML entities?

HTML entities are character representations used in HTML. They can be written with a named form, such as &lt;, or a numeric form, such as &#60;. Browsers interpret these representations and display the corresponding characters.

For example, the less-than character < is commonly represented as &lt;. This is useful because the less-than character also has a special role in HTML tags.

What does an HTML entity encoder do?

An HTML entity encoder converts selected special characters into their entity forms. Websitewire's encoder replaces these five characters:

  • & becomes &amp;
  • < becomes &lt;
  • > becomes &gt;
  • " becomes &quot;
  • ' becomes &#39;

Other characters are left unchanged by this tool.

What does an HTML entity decoder do?

A decoder converts HTML entity representations back into their corresponding characters. For example, decoding &lt;h1&gt; produces <h1>. This is helpful when you receive encoded text and want to read or reuse its original characters.
